Privacy Policy

Last updated: 2026-10-09

quine runs as an Android app and in your web browser (the browser version is served from this website). This policy explains what happens to your data in both. It is written to be plain and honest, not to be exhaustive legalese.

No backend, no account, no analytics

quine has no server operated by its developer. There is no quine account, and quine contains no analytics and no third-party tracking. The "worlds" you create and your conversations with the assistant are stored on your device only. The only data the developer ever receives is a report you choose to send (see "Reporting AI content"):

  • in the Android app, in the app's private files;
  • in the browser, in this website's private storage inside your browser (the Origin Private File System). Clearing this site's data in your browser deletes them.

This website is static files hosted by GitHub Pages. Like any web server, GitHub receives your IP address when your browser loads it; see the GitHub Privacy Statement. The quine developer receives no data from this.

Your OpenRouter key

The assistant runs on an AI service reached through OpenRouter, using your own OpenRouter API key. You get it by signing in with your OpenRouter account, or you paste a key you created yourself. Signing in happens in your browser on openrouter.ai; OpenRouter then hands quine an API key that belongs to your account, and quine never sees your OpenRouter password. You can revoke the key at any time in your OpenRouter account. To show the list of available models, quine also loads OpenRouter's public model list, without sending your key.

  • In the Android app, the key is encrypted on your device with AES-GCM under a non-extractable key held in the Android KeyStore, and only the encrypted result is stored, in the app's private preferences.
  • In the browser, the key, the chosen model and the reasoning effort are stored in this website's local storage in your browser. That storage is not encrypted; anyone with access to your browser profile can read it. Remove the key in quine's Settings page or clear this site's data to delete it.

The key is sent only to OpenRouter. It is never written to your worlds, your conversations or logs. Removing the key in quine's Settings deletes it from your device only; it stays valid until you revoke it in your OpenRouter account. The Android app also has hidden developer options, which you unlock yourself in Settings; one of them is an unreleased sign-in with a ChatGPT account. If you turn it on and use it, the assistant's requests go to OpenAI instead, under OpenAI's privacy policy.

What is sent to the AI service

When you use the assistant, the following are sent to OpenRouter and the AI model provider you selected, so they can generate a response:

  • your messages to the assistant, typed or dictated,
  • voice messages you record for the assistant,
  • photos and audio files you attach to a message,
  • the content of the world the assistant is working on, including images of quine's own screen that the assistant looks at,
  • web searches the assistant makes (see "Web access").

Along with your messages quine tells the assistant which device it runs on (device model, Android version and quine version). Each app also has a random identifier that is sent to OpenRouter with every request, so that the requests of one app belong together; it is not tied to you or your device. Apps you build can ask the AI themselves, for example to write text or draw a picture, and their content goes to OpenRouter the same way.

In the browser, these requests go directly from your browser to OpenRouter. This data is handled under the privacy policies of OpenRouter and the respective model provider; see OpenRouter's privacy policy. The quine developer does not receive or store it.

Attachments and voice messages

A photo, audio file or voice message you attach is stored inside that app on your device, as part of its conversation with the assistant. It is sent to the AI service with the request it belongs to, and again with later requests while it is part of the conversation the assistant reads, so the assistant keeps seeing what you showed it. Deleting that app in quine, or uninstalling quine, deletes it.

Camera and microphone inside your apps

An app you build can take a photo or record sound, but only when you tap or press a key to start it. The photo or recording is stored inside that app on your device, and it is sent to the AI service only if the app or the assistant includes it in a request. Taking photos uses your device's camera app; recording sound needs the microphone permission.

Speech recognition

Dictation, and an app that asks you to speak, use your device's speech recognition service. It needs the microphone permission and may process the audio on your device or on the servers of its provider (usually Google), under that provider's privacy policy. quine receives only the recognised text, and it listens only while that world is visible on your screen.

Web access

The assistant and the apps it builds can load web pages and call web services on your behalf, for example to look something up. Those websites receive the request and your IP address, under their own privacy policies. An app can also send data you put into it to public websites; quine only makes such requests over encrypted (https) connections. Web searches are sent through OpenRouter, using your key, to the search engine it uses (currently Exa). In the Android app web access can be switched off in Settings. In the browser, such requests are sent by your browser and only reach sites that allow it.

Sharing an app

When you share, save or export an app, the file contains the whole app: its content, everything stored inside it (for example photos or recordings it took) and its conversation with the assistant, including the photos, audio files and voice messages you attached. The file also names the device it was made on (model and Android version). Whoever receives the file can read all of it. Nothing is shared unless you choose to.

Reporting AI content

In the Android app you can report a message from the assistant, or an app's AI-generated content, to the quine developer. Only when you send a report, quine transmits: what you reported (a message or an app), the reason you picked, your optional note, the text of the reported assistant message, the name of the AI model in use and quine's version number. Your messages, attachments, apps and keys are never included, except what you write in the note and what the reported assistant message itself quotes. Reports are delivered through Google Forms, so Google also receives your IP address, under the Google Privacy Policy. The developer uses reports only to improve how quine prevents harmful content. A reported assistant message is hidden in its conversation on your device.

Permissions

The Android app asks for: internet access, to reach OpenRouter and the web; the microphone, only when you record a voice message, dictate, or start a recording in an app; and notifications, to show "Agent is generating…" while the assistant answers. While it answers, the app keeps running briefly in the background (an Android foreground service) so the answer arrives; it stops when the answer is done. quine does not ask for the camera permission: photos are taken by your device's camera app. quine never reads your location, contacts or files, except those you pick yourself to attach or import.

Deleting your data

  • On your device: delete an app in quine (this removes its content, its conversation, its attachments and its logs) or uninstall quine (this removes everything). In the browser, clear this site's data.
  • At OpenRouter and the AI model providers: their policies apply. You can revoke your key in your OpenRouter account.
  • Reports: they are stored by the developer in a Google Sheet, without anything that identifies you. To have one deleted, write to quine@felx.me and quote its text.
  • Files you shared or saved: copies outside quine are out of its reach.

Children

quine is not directed at children.
